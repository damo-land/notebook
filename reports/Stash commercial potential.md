# Sell Stash to Obsidian users, not everyone

Stash as it exists today (Mac-only, local-only, choose-a-folder setup, AI through the user's own Claude subscription token or Ollama) is not a sellable product for a non-technical audience. Its signature AI path is also not allowed for a distributed app: Anthropic's terms bar third-party developers from routing requests through Free, Pro or Max credentials on behalf of their users ([Claude Code Legal and compliance](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance)). The capture overlay on its own is already a commodity. Apple's Spotlight Quick Keys and Siri AI cover it for free, and a cluster of Obsidian capture apps sells for $0–8, one of which already offers AI Q&A over the vault ([capture.surf](https://www.capture.surf/)). Turning Stash into something mainstream users expect (sync that just works, an iOS app with share-sheet and widget capture, zero-setup AI) would take an estimated two to five months of solo development (9–22 weeks, per the researchers' unsourced estimates). It would then compete head-on with free Apple Notes and Reminders and with subscription suites that have teams and funding. The realistic opportunity is narrower. Stash can be a paid Obsidian and Mac-power-user companion whose real differentiator is markdown-native tasks with deadlines and alerts plus AI that writes tags and links back into the vault. That points to a one-time license of about $15–25, an optional metered AI tier, and community-led distribution. Benchmarked against disclosed indie Mac apps, a realistic base case is **about $1–5K per month**, with **$15K+ per month** only if Stash gets an Antinote-style press moment. It works as a sustainable side business, or a modest solo income at best. It is not a venture-scale company.

## The capture overlay is already free, so Stash has to win on tasks and vault-aware AI

Stash's core gesture (hotkey, type, Enter) is what Apple shipped in macOS 26 Tahoe in 2025. Spotlight gained an Actions section with more than 100 built-in actions, third-party actions through App Intents, and Quick Keys such as "ar" to add a reminder, all completed inside the Spotlight panel ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/06/09/apple-supercharges-spotlight-in-macos-tahoe-with-quick-keys-and-more/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/09/apple-updates-spotlight-to-take-actions-on-your-mac/)). The current release, **macOS 27 "Golden Gate" (shipped 2026-09-14)**, goes further and turns Spotlight into a "Search or Ask" bar backed by Siri AI with personal context and multi-app actions ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/09/14/apple-releases-macos-golden-gate/)). Siri AI is still a beta that runs only in English, in certain regions, and on modern hardware ([Things Blog](https://culturedcode.com/things/blog/2026/09/things-for-os-27-and-siri-ai/)). Reviewers say Spotlight is "much better than before" but not as capable as Raycast ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/14/macos-27-new-siri-takes-on-ai-productivity-apps/)). Mac power users expect casual users to find it "good enough" ([MPU Talk](https://talk.macpowerusers.com/t/so-spotlight-in-macos-tahoe-is-almost-raycast-alfred/40947)). That second judgment is the one that matters for a non-technical audience. Spotlight plus Apple Reminders and Notes is free, synced and already on the user's phone, and Reminders now suggests and auto-sorts tasks with Apple Intelligence ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/10/13/heres-everything-new-in-reminders-with-ios-26/)).

The indie field crowds the same space. **Antinote** sells a hotkey overlay that works even over fullscreen apps, is local-only with no accounts, and costs **$5 one-time** ([antinote.io](https://antinote.io/)). **Capture** costs **$7.99**, saves to Obsidian on ⌥C, and already ships beta "AI Answers" over the vault with citations ([capture.surf](https://www.capture.surf/)). **Quick Capture** by Practical PKM is pay-what-you-want and writes thoughts, journal entries and tasks into a vault even when Obsidian is closed ([Quick Capture](https://quickcapture.practicalpkm.com/)). Open-source projects already pair Obsidian capture with Claude Code and Codex chat ([GitHub](https://github.com/lucasXiaofan/quick-capture-macos)). Above them sits **Raycast**, funded with a $30M Series B ([Raycast Blog](https://www.raycast.com/blog/series-b)). Its official pricing page shows a free tier capped at 5 notes, and Pro at **$10/mo or $96/yr** with unlimited notes, cloud sync, AI, and BYOK including local models. Credit-based Plus and Max tiers sit at $20 and $50/mo ([Raycast Pricing](https://www.raycast.com/pricing)). Some secondary notes cite an older $8–10 / $16–20 Raycast structure. The official page supersedes those figures. Raycast also has a live iOS app that syncs notes and AI chats on Pro ([Raycast iOS](https://www.raycast.com/ios)).

For Stash's AI layer, the strongest substitute is the free Obsidian plugin stack. **Copilot for Obsidian** has about **2.4M downloads** and offers vault chat. **Smart Connections** has about **1.2M** and gives local semantic linking with "no API key" ([Copilot](https://community.obsidian.md/plugins/copilot); [Smart Connections](https://community.obsidian.md/plugins/smart-connections)). Platforms bundle "AI over your notes" into products people already pay for. Notion folded its AI add-on into Business ([Carly](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/notion-ai-pricing-change/)). NotebookLM's paid tier dropped to $4.99/mo inside Google AI plans ([Sourclip](https://www.sourclip.com/blog/notebooklm-pricing)). Claude memory has been free for all users since March 2026 ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/03/02/anthropic-memory-import-tool/)). The history of AI-first notes companies is a warning. Mem raised about $29M on "AI organizes your notes" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/10/ai-powered-note-taking-app-mem-raises-23-5m-openai/)), then spent about two years rebuilding and relaunched in October 2025 as an "AI Thought Partner" ([Mem blog](https://get.mem.ai/blog/introducing-mem-2-0)). Reor, a local AI notes app with auto-linking and Ollama, was archived in March 2026 ([GitHub](https://github.com/reorproject/reor)). Khoj shut down its hosted cloud in April 2026 ([Khoj](https://app.khoj.dev/)).

No single product combines all of Stash's pieces: a global overlay, plain markdown in an Obsidian-compatible folder, tasks with real deadlines and native macOS notifications, and AI that writes tags and wiki-links back into the files. The capture micro-apps stop at appending text. Task apps with strong quick entry (Things, Todoist, Superlist) keep data in proprietary cloud stores ([Things Blog](https://culturedcode.com/things/blog/2026/09/things-for-os-27-and-siri-ai/); [Superlist Help](https://help.superlist.com/en/articles/310866-superlist-pricing-free-basic-and-super-plans-compared)). NotePlan comes closest on markdown plus tasks but costs about $99/yr and runs its own sync ([xpay.sh](https://www.xpay.sh/saas-pricing/noteplan-co/)). Stash's defensible position is therefore markdown-native task management with alerts, plus vault enrichment. The hotkey panel and AI chat on their own are not defensible, because others ship them free or cheaply and can copy them quickly.

## Disclosed indie revenue points to a niche worth thousands per month, not millions

Market-research totals do not help here. Estimates for the 2025 note-taking app market range from **$1.35B to $17.19B**, a 12x spread built mostly on enterprise SaaS ([MRFR](https://www.marketresearchfuture.com/reports/note-taking-app-market-27737); [DataIntelo](https://dataintelo.com/report/global-note-taking-app-market)). A bottom-up view is more honest. An aggregator estimates all third-party Mac software at **$3–6B a year**, with average Mac software spend of $20–50 per user per year, and calls the Mac market about 15x smaller than the iOS App Store ([macapps.report](https://macapps.report/market)). Apple last disclosed **100M active Macs** in 2018 ([AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/18/10/30/apple-passes-100m-active-mac-milestone-thanks-to-high-numbers-of-new-users)). No source says what share of those users buy indie utilities. The real addressable pool is a small and unmeasured minority: Mac power users, plus the roughly 1–1.5M Obsidian users. That Obsidian figure is itself unconfirmed ([OperatorBook](https://www.operatorbook.dev/stories/obsidian-revenue-estimates-2m-to-25m)).

Disclosed outcomes for comparable apps cluster tightly. **Reflect**, a notes app with about four staff, reported "just over $30K MRR" from 2,500+ customers ([Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/reflect-80abf444-ccfc-414a-99d1-4eb23d28d181)). Founder figures compiled by secondary sources put **BoltAI** (a solo-built, BYOK Mac AI client) at $15–30K/month in 2025. The same compilation puts Xnapper at about $4K/month before its sale and RocketSim at about $62K ARR ([stealwhatworks](https://stealwhatworks.com/blogs/news/mac-apps-making-real-money-now); [macapps.report](https://macapps.report/market)). **MacWhisper** earned about **$100K in total** on lifetime licenses from 57,574 downloads ([Startups email](https://startupsemail.substack.com/p/100000-revenue-with-a-simple-mac)). That works out to roughly $1.74 per download, a useful real-world yardstick for a one-time Mac app. Most apps do far worse. RevenueCat finds that only **17.3%** of new subscription apps reach $1K MRR, and only **4.6%** reach $10K MRR, within two years ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)). The capture niche itself looks thin. The Iconfactory, maker of the beloved capture app Tot, put several apps up for sale in 2025, saying people "aren't subscribing in great enough numbers" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/31/design-and-development-shop-the-iconfactory-is-selling-some-apps-and-ai-is-partially-to-blame/)). Even Obsidian, the category's clear winner, has never published revenue. Estimates run from **$2M to $25M ARR** ([OperatorBook](https://www.operatorbook.dev/stories/obsidian-revenue-estimates-2m-to-25m)).

Willingness to pay splits sharply by product type. Capture tools sell for **$0–8 one-time**. Tot appears both as a free Mac app with a $20 iOS version ([Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/233920/tot-pocket-review.html)) and as a $19.99 one-time in-app purchase on the Mac App Store ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tot/id1491071483?mt=12)). Notes apps with sync charge $20–30 a year: Drafts Pro is $19.99/yr and Bear Pro is $29.99/yr ([Drafts Pro](https://docs.getdrafts.com/draftspro); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/bear-markdown-notes/id1016366447)). Suites that combine AI and sync reach $96–120+ a year. RevenueCat's productivity benchmarks show **5.9%** download-to-trial, **$0.50 revenue per install** at day 60, and **$24.95** realized first-year value per payer. **72%** of annual plans are cancelled within the first year ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps); [RevenueCat blog](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)). AI apps earn 41% more first-year value but retain 36% worse ([RevenueCat blog](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)). Two caveats apply. These figures come mostly from mobile apps, and Mac direct-download buyers who arrive from niche press probably convert better, though no source has measured it. Meanwhile, 41% of consumers report subscription fatigue ([Adapty](https://adapty.io/glossary/subscription-fatigue/)). Mac utilities now advertise "no subscription" as a feature ([screensnap.pro](https://www.screensnap.pro/blog/best-cleanshot-x-alternative-in-2026-plus-4-more-options-for-mac-users)).

## Mainstream users would need sync, an iOS app and zero-setup AI, and the subscription-token path cannot ship

Non-technical users judge a notes app against Apple Notes. It offers capture from the Lock Screen, Siri and the share sheet, with sync nobody has to configure. Writers who left Obsidian cite the gap between "I need to write something down" and "I've written it down" ([XDA](https://www.xda-developers.com/stopped-using-obsidian-for-quick-notes-and-vault-got-more-useful/)). They call free sync workarounds "a lot more effort than it's worth for quick captures" ([Medium](https://oscarlagrosen.medium.com/why-i-switched-from-obsidian-to-apple-notes-5e53088eb879)). Letting iCloud Drive sync the folder is the cheap answer, and it fails in ways ordinary users cannot diagnose. "Optimize Mac Storage" evicts files, so search and backlinks silently miss notes. Concurrent edits produce "Note 2.md" duplicates that iCloud never merges ([Eira](https://eirasync.app/blog/why-icloud-creates-obsidian-sync-conflicts); [Obsidian Forum](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/about-icloud-sync-and-mac-optimized-storage/43282)). Three parts of Stash today each rule it out for a mainstream buyer: a folder picker on first run, no phone app, and AI that needs a terminal command or an Ollama install.

**The AI path needs replacing before any public launch.** Two of the research notes list "uses the user's existing Claude subscription" as a differentiator. The primary source overrides them. Anthropic's Claude Code legal page states that it "does not permit third-party developers to offer Claude.ai login into their own applications, or to route requests through Free, Pro, or Max plan credentials on behalf of their users." It adds that developers "may not collect, store, or intermediate Claude.ai credentials or session tokens," and that products "should use API key authentication" ([Claude Code Legal and compliance](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance)). Stash's current flow has the user run `claude setup-token` and pass the token to a Node sidecar, which is exactly what that clause describes. The policy is actively enforced. Enforcement against third-party clients using subscription OAuth began on 9 January 2026. The docs were clarified on 18–19 February 2026 to name the Agent SDK, and the section was rewritten again on 7 October 2026 ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/software/2026/02/20/anthropic-clarifies-ban-on-third-party-tool-access-to-claude/5014546); [WinBuzzer](https://winbuzzer.com/2026/02/19/anthropic-bans-claude-subscription-oauth-in-third-party-apps-xcxwbn/)). The page carves out one narrow exception: hosting an unmodified Claude Code binary where each user signs in through Anthropic's own flow. That exception is written for hosting Claude Code, not for a notes app's background enrichment, so it does not rescue the feature. Stash can keep the subscription-token path as a personal tool, but it cannot ship as a feature of a commercial product. The compliant alternatives are below.

| AI path | Cost to developer | Fit for non-technical users | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apple Foundation Models (on-device) | $0 | High, with no setup | About 3B parameters, **4,096-token** context, requires Apple silicon with Apple Intelligence on macOS 26+ ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/806542); [InfoQ](https://infoq.com/news/2026/03/apple-foundation-models-context)). Fits tagging and task extraction on single notes, not chat over the whole vault. |
| Hosted, metered AI via the developer's API key | ~$0.05/mo typical, ~$0.45/mo heavy user on Haiku 5.5 (researcher arithmetic at $0.10/$0.50 per MTok) ([Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)) | High | Needs sign-in, a proxy, per-user credit caps and abuse controls. Vault-wide chat costs 10–100x more per action. The notes give no effort estimate for building the proxy. |
| Setapp AI Gateway | Billed through MacPaw Wallet | Medium | A unified API across OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and local models, launched May 2026 ([Setapp news](https://setapp.com/news/company-updates)). Could avoid running AI billing, but is unverified for this use. |
| BYO API key | $0 | Low | Allowed, because usage bills to the key owner. Accepted by power users (BoltAI, MacWhisper), but pasting a key is a barrier for mainstream buyers. |
| Ollama (local) | $0 | Very low | Private and free. Requires installing a separate runtime. |

The rest of the mainstream build costs time rather than infrastructure. Every effort figure below is the researcher's own estimate for one experienced developer, with no source behind it. Storage and compute are cheap. A CloudKit private database costs the developer nothing, because usage counts against each user's iCloud quota ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/665612)). A custom sync service on Cloudflare Durable Objects starts at about $5/mo ([Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/)), and Supabase Pro is $25/mo ([UI Bakery](https://uibakery.io/blog/supabase-pricing)). For text-only notes, either works out to well under a cent per user per month. Building it is the expensive part. Estimates are about **3–6 weeks** for CloudKit sync through a Swift bridge from Tauri, or **4–8 weeks** for a custom backend with conflict handling, plus **2–4 weeks** for end-to-end encryption that matches Obsidian Sync's privacy claims ([Obsidian pricing](https://obsidian.md/pricing)). An iOS companion adds roughly **6–10 weeks** in SwiftUI. Tauri v2 can reach the App Store and reuse the React UI ([Tauri docs](https://v2.tauri.app/distribute/app-store/)), but its iOS widget support is still an open request, and share extensions need separate Xcode targets ([Tauri issue #9766](https://github.com/tauri-apps/tauri/issues/9766); [tauri-apple-extensions](https://github.com/Choochmeque/tauri-apple-extensions)). That puts a Tauri iOS app at about 4–8 weeks plus 2–4 weeks of native work, and it risks the "desktop app shrunk down" feel users already criticize in Obsidian mobile ([Medium](https://wyuhuan.medium.com/why-i-left-obsidian-and-notion-for-a-markdown-note-taking-app-built-for-the-phone-not-around-it-d1e679f4a6f2)). Together, the mainstream version needs an estimated **9–22 weeks** (CloudKit sync plus a SwiftUI companion at the low end; custom E2E-encrypted sync plus iOS at the high end), plus a hosted-AI proxy of unknown effort, plus permanent support and on-call load. The result would then compete with Apple Notes, which is free, and with Raycast, which has an iOS app and $30M of funding.

On distribution, a direct download with Developer ID and notarization is the right call for the Mac app. The sandbox allows global hotkeys through `RegisterEventHotKey` ([Michael Tsai](https://mjtsai.com/blog/2012/05/17/hotkeys-and-the-mac-app-store/)), but it blocks Accessibility APIs ([DEV](https://dev.to/eduardo_revillavaquero_0/i-shipped-a-clipboard-manager-inside-the-macos-app-store-sandbox-heres-what-works-and-what-5a30)), and a bundled Node sidecar adds review risk the research did not resolve. Direct sales also match where Mac revenue is going. Among developers selling through both channels, **52%** of revenue now comes from outside the App Store ([Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/3621224/setapp-survey-reveals-what-mac-developers-are-thinking-about-right-now.html)).

## A one-time license plus metered AI fits the evidence, and Obsidian communities are the market to reach

Comparable apps point to the same model. Small Mac utilities earn from the app itself, either cheap and one-time or pay-what-you-want. Recurring revenue comes only from a service with real running costs, such as Obsidian Sync, CleanShot Cloud or Raycast Pro ([Obsidian pricing](https://obsidian.md/pricing); [stackscored](https://www.stackscored.com/pricing/screen-recording/cleanshot-x/)). Stash has no sync to sell, so its one natural recurring product is hosted AI. Pricing a one-time app with bundled cloud AI is a margin trap, and Raycast moved to usage-based credits for that reason ([Raycast blog](https://www.raycast.com/blog/changing-how-raycast-ai-is-priced)). The model that fits has three layers:

- **Core license.** About **$15–25 one-time**, with free on-device Apple Foundation Models enrichment included. That is at or above the top of the capture band and well under Things for Mac at $49.99 ([ellieplanner](https://ellieplanner.com/productivity-copilot/things-3-pricing)). Tasks, alerts and vault enrichment justify the premium over the $5–8 apps.
- **Optional "Stash AI" subscription.** About **$4–6/mo** for hosted vault chat and higher-quality enrichment, with hard monthly credit caps, priced well under Raycast Pro.
- **Power-user options.** BYO API key and Ollama, free and unmetered.

Sell direct through a merchant of record. Paddle charges 5% + $0.50 and absorbs chargebacks ([swell.is](https://www.swell.is/content/lemon-squeezy-pricing)). Setapp is a useful secondary channel. It pays about 70% of membership revenue by usage, and since March 2026 it also sells individual apps ([Setapp docs](https://docs.setapp.com/docs/setapp-membership-revenue); [Setapp developers](https://setapp.com/developers)). Developers have historically reported anywhere from under 1% to about a fifth of their revenue from it ([TidBITS](https://tidbits.com/2017/06/29/setapp-at-5-months-10000-users-and-better-app-discovery/); [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/22/06/17/how-setapps-app-service-is-building-value-for-developers-and-users)).

For go-to-market, press and communities matter more than launch platforms. Antinote's adoption came from The Verge's "Top 10 Favorite Things of 2025," Digital Trends and Lifehacker, plus Reddit word of mouth ([antinote.io](https://antinote.io/); [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/computing/i-tried-a-5-scratchpad-app-for-the-mac-ill-never-go-back-to-apple-notes/)). Tot grew through MacStories and Daring Fireball coverage ([MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/reviews/tot-review-collect-and-edit-bits-of-text/)). Product Hunt in 2026 is dominated by Anthropic, Google and Vercel, and "a true indy project has little hope" ([molodtsov.me](https://molodtsov.me/2026/04/we-need-a-new-product-hunt/)). The demand Stash can most easily reach is in Obsidian's own forum, where users have long asked for global-hotkey capture because the app "lacks in the quick capture department" ([Obsidian forum](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/quick-capture-prompt/43424); [QuickAdd #238](https://github.com/chhoumann/quickadd/issues/238)). Stash should also expose App Intents so that "Add to Stash" appears in Spotlight and Siri. Things 3.24 did exactly this for macOS 27 ([Things Blog](https://culturedcode.com/things/blog/2026/09/things-for-os-27-and-siri-ai/)), and it turns Apple's capture surface from a threat into a channel.

The main risks follow from this positioning:

- **Commoditization.** The capture layer is already free or $5–8.
- **Sherlocking.** Apple keeps extending Spotlight, Siri AI and Reminders.
- **Obsidian or Raycast shipping the same features.** No announcement exists, but both own the audience.
- **Compliance.** AI that leans on a third party's terms can be shut off "without prior notice" ([Claude Code Legal and compliance](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance)).
- **Retention.** RevenueCat's churn data suggests that retention, not acquisition, limits any subscription tier.

## Verdict: a credible side business at $1–5K a month, with upside tied to a press moment

The scenarios below follow the arithmetic of the research notes. Downloads per year are multiplied by the paid conversion rate and the license price. AI subscribers are counted as a share of buyers at about $60 a year, before churn. The conversion rates are assumptions: RevenueCat's **2%** download-to-paid median for mobile apps ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)), stretched upward for higher-intent Mac direct downloads, which no source has measured. Each outcome is checked against a disclosed comparable.

| Scenario | Assumptions | Year-1 revenue | Comparable |
|---|---|---|---|
| Downside: undifferentiated capture app | 5K downloads × 2% × $15; negligible AI tier | ~$1.5K (~$125/mo) | Median indie app under $500/mo ([TechLila](https://www.techlila.com/mac-app-store-vs-direct-downloads-dmg/), unverified secondary) |
| Base: Obsidian companion with tasks and AI | 30K downloads × 4% × $19 ≈ $23K; 10% of buyers on AI ≈ $7K; small Setapp income | ~$30K (~$2.5K/mo) | RocketSim, Xnapper, DisplayBuddy ($1.5–5K/mo) ([macapps.report](https://macapps.report/market)) |
| Upside: press breakout | 150K downloads × 5% × $19 ≈ $140K; ~1,000 AI subscribers ≈ $60K | ~$200K (~$15–17K/mo) | BoltAI and Reflect ($15–30K/mo) ([stealwhatworks](https://stealwhatworks.com/blogs/news/mac-apps-making-real-money-now); [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/reflect-80abf444-ccfc-414a-99d1-4eb23d28d181)) |
| Mainstream pivot (sync + iOS + subscription) | 100K downloads × 2% × $24.95 first-year value per payer; after an estimated 9–22 weeks of extra build | ~$50K, before support costs | RevenueCat productivity benchmarks; Iconfactory's subscription struggles |

The last row is the most important result. Even with a large download base, the mainstream subscription version earns about what the base companion case earns, after months more work, ongoing infrastructure and support, and direct competition with Apple's free apps. The extra build does not raise expected revenue. It mainly adds risk. The recommended path is to:

- Replace the subscription-token path before any public release.
- Ship the Mac app direct, with free on-device enrichment and an optional metered hosted-AI tier.
- Position Stash as "the inbox and task layer for your Obsidian vault": hotkey capture, deadlines and Mac alerts in plain markdown, AI that tags and links your notes, and nothing leaving your Mac unless you choose.
- Distribute through Obsidian communities, PKM creators, Mac newsletters and r/macapps.
- Build iOS and sync only if the base case materializes and buyers ask for them.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. It is not whether non-technical people want a quick-capture app; Apple has already given them one for free. It is whether a narrow, reachable audience will pay for a feature nobody else sells: tasks and AI enrichment that live in their own markdown files. That audience exists and is visible in Obsidian's feature requests and in the many apps built to fill the gap. It is small, though, it is price-anchored at single-digit one-time prices, and its AI needs are already partly met by free plugins with millions of installs. Stash's commercial ceiling is set less by its feature list than by distribution luck and by how quickly its most differentiated parts (markdown-native deadlines, notifications and write-back enrichment) get copied.

One insight runs against intuition. The mainstream route looks like the bigger market, but it is the worse bet for a solo developer, because it swaps Stash's only structural advantages (no backend, no per-user cost, file ownership) for Apple's home turf. The AI compliance issue is a good forcing function. Rebuilding enrichment on on-device Foundation Models removes per-user cost and makes "private by default" a literal claim rather than a slogan. It also gives the paid tier a clear reason to exist, as the place where heavier, hosted vault-wide reasoning lives.
